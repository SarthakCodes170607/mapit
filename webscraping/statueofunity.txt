<p>
The <i><b>Statue of Unity</b></i> is the world's <a href="/wiki/List_of_tallest_statues" title="List of tallest statues">tallest statue</a>, with a height of 182 metres (597 feet),<sup id="cite&#95;ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>3<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup> located in <a href="/wiki/Narmada_River" title="Narmada River">Narmada valley</a>, near <a href="/wiki/Kevadia" title="Kevadia">Kevadia</a> in the state of <a href="/wiki/Gujarat" title="Gujarat">Gujarat</a>, <a href="/wiki/India" title="India">India</a>. It depicts Indian politician and <a href="/wiki/Indian_independence_movement" title="Indian independence movement">independence</a> activist <a href="/wiki/Vallabhbhai_Patel" title="Vallabhbhai Patel">Sardar Patel</a> (1875–1950), who was the first <a href="/wiki/Deputy_Prime_Minister_of_India" title="Deputy Prime Minister of India">deputy prime minister</a> and <a href="/wiki/Minister_of_Home_Affairs_(India)" title="Minister of Home Affairs (India)">home minister</a> of independent India. Patel played a significant role in the <a href="/wiki/Political_integration_of_India" title="Political integration of India">political integration of India</a>. The statue is on the <a href="/wiki/Narmada_River" title="Narmada River">Narmada River</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Kevadiya" class="mw-redirect" title="Kevadiya">Kevadiya</a> colony, facing the <a href="/wiki/Sardar_Sarovar_Dam" title="Sardar Sarovar Dam">Sardar Sarovar Dam</a>, 100 kilometres (62 miles) southeast of the city of <a href="/wiki/Vadodara" title="Vadodara">Vadodara</a>.<sup id="cite&#95;ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>4<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup>
</p><p>The project was first announced in 2010 by <a href="/wiki/Narendra_Modi" title="Narendra Modi">Narendra Modi</a>, then <a href="/wiki/Chief_Minister_of_Gujarat" title="Chief Minister of Gujarat">Chief Minister of Gujarat</a>, and construction started in October 2013 by Indian company <a href="/wiki/Larsen_%26_Toubro" title="Larsen &amp; Toubro">Larsen &amp; Toubro</a>, with a total construction cost of <a href="/wiki/Indian_rupee" title="Indian rupee">₹</a>27&#160;billion (US$422&#160;million).<sup id="cite&#95;ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>5<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup> It was designed by Indian sculptor <a href="/wiki/Ram_V._Sutar" title="Ram V. Sutar">Ram V. Sutar</a> and was inaugurated by Modi, then <a href="/wiki/Prime_Minister_of_India" title="Prime Minister of India">Prime Minister of India</a>, on 31 October 2018, on what would have been Patel's 143rd birthday.<sup id="cite&#95;ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>6<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<div class="mw-heading mw-heading2"><h2 id="History">History</h2></div>
<p>Then-chief minister of Gujarat <a href="/wiki/Narendra_Modi" title="Narendra Modi">Narendra Modi</a> announced the project on 7 October 2013 to mark the beginning of his tenth year as the chief minister of the state.<sup id="cite&#95;ref-iedate&#95;7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-iedate-7"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>7<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup>
</p><p>A society named Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel Rashtriya Ekta Trust (SVPRET) was formed under the chairmanship of the <a href="/wiki/Chief_Minister_of_Gujarat" title="Chief Minister of Gujarat">Chief Minister of Gujarat</a>, to execute the project.<sup id="cite&#95;ref-iedate&#95;7-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-iedate-7"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>7<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup><sup id="cite&#95;ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>8<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup>
</p><p>The Statue of Unity Movement was started in 2013 to collect the iron needed for the statue by asking farmers to donate their used farming instruments.<sup id="cite&#95;ref-iedate&#95;7-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-iedate-7"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>7<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup><sup id="cite&#95;ref-newindianexpress.com&#95;9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-newindianexpress.com-9"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>9<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup> By 2016, a total of 135&#160;metric&#160;tonnes of scrap iron had been collected and about 109&#160;tonnes of it was used to make the foundation of the statue after processing.<sup id="cite&#95;ref-ironman2.0&#95;10-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ironman2.0-10"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>10<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup> A marathon titled <i>Run For Unity</i> was held on 15 December 2013 in <a href="/wiki/Surat" title="Surat">Surat</a> and <a href="/wiki/Vadodara" title="Vadodara">Vadodara</a> in support of the project.<sup id="cite&#95;ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>11<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup>
</p>
<div class="mw-heading mw-heading2"><h2 id="Design_and_construction">Design and construction</h2></div>

<div class="mw-heading mw-heading3"><h3 id="Design">Design</h3></div>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Praful_Patel,_the_Minister_of_State_(Independent_Charge)_for_Micro,_Small_and_Medium_Enterprises,_Shri_Dinsha_J._Patel_and_other_dignitaries_at_the_unveiling_ceremony_of_the_statue_of_Sardar_Vallabh_Bhai_Patel.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/05/Praful_Patel%2C_the_Minister_of_State_%28Independent_Charge%29_for_Micro%2C_Small_and_Medium_Enterprises%2C_Shri_Dinsha_J._Patel_and_other_dignitaries_at_the_unveiling_ceremony_of_the_statue_of_Sardar_Vallabh_Bhai_Patel.jpg/250px-thumbnail.jpg" decoding="async" width="250" height="325" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/05/Praful_Patel%2C_the_Minister_of_State_%28Independent_Charge%29_for_Micro%2C_Small_and_Medium_Enterprises%2C_Shri_Dinsha_J._Patel_and_other_dignitaries_at_the_unveiling_ceremony_of_the_statue_of_Sardar_Vallabh_Bhai_Patel.jpg/500px-thumbnail.jpg 2x" data-file-width="2200" data-file-height="2860" /></a><figcaption>The <i>Statue of Unity</i> is an enlarged version of this statue at the <a href="/wiki/Ahmedabad_International_Airport" class="mw-redirect" title="Ahmedabad International Airport">Ahmedabad International Airport</a>.</figcaption></figure>
<p>After studying statues of Patel across the country a team of historians, artists, and academics chose a design submitted by the Indian sculptor <a href="/wiki/Ram_V._Sutar" title="Ram V. Sutar">Ram V. Sutar</a>.<sup id="cite&#95;ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>a<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup> The <i>Statue of Unity</i> is a larger version of a statue of the leader installed at <a href="/wiki/Ahmedabad_International_Airport" class="mw-redirect" title="Ahmedabad International Airport">Ahmedabad International Airport</a>. Three models of the design measuring 0.91&#160;metres (3&#160;ft), 5.5 metres (18&#160;ft), and 9.1 metres (30&#160;ft) were initially created. Once the design of the largest model was approved, a detailed <a href="/wiki/3D_scanning" title="3D scanning">3D scan</a> was produced which formed the basis for the <a href="/wiki/Bronze" title="Bronze">bronze</a> cladding cast in a <a href="/wiki/Foundry" title="Foundry">foundry</a> in China.<sup id="cite&#95;ref-ironman2.0&#95;10-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ironman2.0-10"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>10<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup><sup id="cite&#95;ref-HTMaking&#95;15-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-HTMaking-15"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>14<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup>
</p><p>The design showing Patel's <a href="/wiki/Dhoti" title="Dhoti">dhoti</a>-clad legs and sandalled feet make the statue narrower at the base with a slenderness ratio that varies between 16 and 19, significantly higher than most tall buildings with ratios between 8 and 14.<sup id="cite&#95;ref-CTBUH&#95;2015&#95;16-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CTBUH_2015-16"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>15<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup> This presents a challenge to the stability which was addressed in part through the use of two 250-tonne <a href="/wiki/Tuned_mass_damper" title="Tuned mass damper">tuned mass dampers</a>.<sup id="cite&#95;ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>16<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup><sup id="cite&#95;ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>17<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup> The statue is built to withstand winds of up to 180 kilometres per hour (110&#160;mph) and earthquakes measuring 6.5 on the <a href="/wiki/Richter_scale" title="Richter scale">Richter scale</a> which are at a depth of 10&#160;km and within a radius of 12&#160;km of the statue.<sup id="cite&#95;ref-ibn&#95;1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ibn-1"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>1<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup><sup id="cite&#95;ref-ironman2.0&#95;10-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ironman2.0-10"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>10<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup>
</p><p>The total height of the structure is 240&#160;m (790&#160;ft), with a base of 58&#160;m (190&#160;ft) and the statue measuring 182&#160;m (597&#160;ft).<sup id="cite&#95;ref-ibn&#95;1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ibn-1"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>1<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup> The height of 182&#160;metres was specifically chosen to match the number of seats in the <a href="/wiki/Gujarat_Legislative_Assembly" title="Gujarat Legislative Assembly">Gujarat Legislative Assembly</a>.<sup id="cite&#95;ref-iedate&#95;7-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-iedate-7"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>7<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup><sup id="cite&#95;ref-ironman2.0&#95;10-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ironman2.0-10"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>10<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup>
</p>
<div class="mw-heading mw-heading3"><h3 id="Funding">Funding</h3></div>
<p>The <i>Statue of Unity</i> was built by a <a href="/wiki/Public%E2%80%93private_partnerships_in_India" title="Public–private partnerships in India">Public-Private Partnership</a>, with most of the money coming from the <a href="/wiki/Government_of_Gujarat" title="Government of Gujarat">Government of Gujarat</a>. The Gujarat state government had allotted <span class="nowrap"><span style="white-space: nowrap">₹</span>500 crore</span> (equivalent to <span style="white-space: nowrap">₹</span>755&#160;crore&#32;or&#32;US$89&#160;million in 2023) for the project in its <a href="/wiki/Government_budget" title="Government budget">budget</a> from 2012 to 2015.<sup id="cite&#95;ref-prj&#95;19-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-prj-19"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>18<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup><sup id="cite&#95;ref-20" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-20"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>19<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup> In the <a href="/wiki/2014_Union_budget_of_India" title="2014 Union budget of India">2014–15 Union Budget</a>, <span class="nowrap"><span style="white-space: nowrap">₹</span>200 crore</span> (equivalent to <span style="white-space: nowrap">₹</span>320&#160;crore&#32;or&#32;US$38&#160;million in 2023) was allocated for the construction of the statue.<sup id="cite&#95;ref-21" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-21"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>20<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup><sup id="cite&#95;ref-22" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-22"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>21<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup><sup id="cite&#95;ref-Cost&#95;Project&#95;23-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Cost_Project-23"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>22<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup> Funds were also contributed by <a href="/wiki/Public_sector_undertakings_in_India" class="mw-redirect" title="Public sector undertakings in India">Public Sector Undertakings</a> under the <a href="/wiki/Corporate_Social_Responsibility" class="mw-redirect" title="Corporate Social Responsibility">Corporate Social Responsibility</a> scheme.<sup id="cite&#95;ref-24" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-24"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>23<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup>
</p>

<p>A consortium comprising <a href="/wiki/Turner_Construction" title="Turner Construction">Turner Construction</a>, <a href="/wiki/Michael_Graves_and_Associates" class="mw-redirect" title="Michael Graves and Associates">Michael Graves and Associates</a> and the Meinhardt Group supervised the project. The project took 57&#160;months to complete&#160;– 15&#160;months for planning, 40&#160;months for construction and 2&#160;months for handing over by the consortium.<sup id="cite&#95;ref-bk&#95;25-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-bk-25"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>24<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup> The total cost of the project was estimated to be about <span class="nowrap"><span style="white-space: nowrap">₹</span>2,063 crore</span> (equivalent to <span style="white-space: nowrap">₹</span>33&#160;billion&#32;or&#32;US$390&#160;million in 2023) by the Government.<sup id="cite&#95;ref-prj&#95;19-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-prj-19"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>18<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup> The tender bids for the first phase were invited in October 2013 and were closed in November 2013.<sup id="cite&#95;ref-26" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-26"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>25<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup>
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Narendra_Modi" title="Narendra Modi">Narendra Modi</a>, then serving as the <a href="/wiki/Chief_Minister_of_Gujarat" title="Chief Minister of Gujarat">Chief Minister of Gujarat</a>, laid the statue's foundation stone on 31 October 2013, the 138th anniversary of Patel's birth.<sup id="cite&#95;ref-BBC-31-Oct-2013&#95;27-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-BBC-31-Oct-2013-27"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>26<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup><sup id="cite&#95;ref-bava&#95;28-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-bava-28"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>27<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup>
</p><p>Indian infrastructure company <a href="/wiki/Larsen_%26_Toubro" title="Larsen &amp; Toubro">Larsen &amp; Toubro</a> won the contract on 27 October 2014 for its lowest bid of <span class="nowrap"><span style="white-space: nowrap">₹</span>2,989 crore</span> (equivalent to <span style="white-space: nowrap">₹</span>48&#160;billion&#32;or&#32;US$560&#160;million in 2023) for the design, construction and maintenance of the statue.<sup id="cite&#95;ref-ixx&#95;29-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ixx-29"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>28<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup><sup id="cite&#95;ref-bsd&#95;30-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-bsd-30"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>29<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Larsen_%26_Toubro" title="Larsen &amp; Toubro">L&amp;T</a> commenced the construction on 31 October 2014. In the first phase of the project, ₹1,347&#160;crore was earmarked for the main statue, ₹235&#160;crore for the exhibition hall and convention centre, ₹83&#160;crore for the bridge connecting the memorial to the mainland and ₹657&#160;crore for the maintenance of the structure for a duration of 15 years after its completion.<sup id="cite&#95;ref-ixx&#95;29-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ixx-29"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>28<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup><sup id="cite&#95;ref-bsd&#95;30-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-bsd-30"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>29<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup> The Sadhu Bet hillock was flattened from 70&#160;metres to 55&#160;metres to lay the foundation of the statue.<sup id="cite&#95;ref-ironman2.0&#95;10-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ironman2.0-10"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>10<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup>
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Larsen_%26_Toubro" title="Larsen &amp; Toubro">L&amp;T</a> employed over 3000 workers and 250 engineers in the statue's construction. The core of the statue used 210,000 cubic metres (7,400,000&#160;ft<sup>3</sup>) of cement and concrete, 6,500&#160;tonnes of structural steel, and 18,500&#160;tonnes of reinforced steel. The outer façade is made up of 1,700&#160;tonnes of bronze plates and 1,850&#160;tonnes of bronze cladding which in turn consists of 565 macro and 6000 micro panels. The bronze panels were cast in Jiangxi Tongqing Metal Handicrafts Co. Ltd (the TQ Art foundry) in China as facilities large enough for such casting were unavailable in India.<sup id="cite&#95;ref-LM31OCT&#95;31-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-LM31OCT-31"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>30<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup><sup id="cite&#95;ref-32" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-32"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>31<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup><sup id="cite&#95;ref-ironman2.0&#95;10-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ironman2.0-10"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>10<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup> The bronze panels were transported over sea and then by road to a workshop near the construction site where they were assembled.<sup id="cite&#95;ref-ironman2.0&#95;10-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ironman2.0-10"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>10<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup>
</p><p>Local tribals belonging to the Tadvi tribe opposed the land acquisition for the development of tourism infrastructure around the statue.<sup id="cite&#95;ref-BBCFarmers&#95;33-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-BBCFarmers-33"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>32<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup> Around 300 activists were arrested ahead of unveiling of the statue.<sup id="cite&#95;ref-activistArrests&#95;34-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-activistArrests-34"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>33<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup> People of Kevadia, Kothi, Waghodia, Limbdi, Navagam, and Gora villages opposed the construction of the statue and demanded the restitution of the land rights over 375 hectares (927 acres) of land acquired earlier for the dam as well as for the formation of a new Garudeshwar <a href="/wiki/Taluka" class="mw-redirect" title="Taluka">subdistrict</a>. They also opposed the formation of the Kevadia Area Development Authority (KADA) and the construction of the Garudeshwar weir-cum-causeway project. The government of Gujarat accepted most of their demands.<sup id="cite&#95;ref-vill&#95;35-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-vill-35"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>34<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup>
</p><p>Construction of the monument was completed in mid-October 2018; and the inaugural ceremony was held on 31 October 2018 (143rd birth anniversary of <a href="/wiki/Vallabhbhai_Patel" title="Vallabhbhai Patel">Vallabhbhai Patel</a>), and was presided over by <a href="/wiki/Prime_Minister_of_India" title="Prime Minister of India">Prime Minister</a> <a href="/wiki/Narendra_Modi" title="Narendra Modi">Narendra Modi</a>.<sup id="cite&#95;ref-36" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-36"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>35<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup><sup id="cite&#95;ref-37" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-37"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>36<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup> The statue has been described as a tribute to Indian engineering skills.<sup id="cite&#95;ref-38" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-38"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>37<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup>
</p>
<div class="mw-heading mw-heading2"><h2 id="Features">Features</h2></div>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Narendra_Modi_visiting_the_Museum,_Exhibition_and_the_Viewers%E2%80%99_Gallery,_during_the_dedication_of_the_%E2%80%98Statue_of_Unity%E2%80%99_to_the_Nation,_on_the_occasion_of_the_Rashtriya_Ekta_Diwas,_at_Kevadiya,_in_Narmada_District_of_Gujarat_(3).JPG" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/e6/Narendra_Modi_visiting_the_Museum%2C_Exhibition_and_the_Viewers%E2%80%99_Gallery%2C_during_the_dedication_of_the_%E2%80%98Statue_of_Unity%E2%80%99_to_the_Nation%2C_on_the_occasion_of_the_Rashtriya_Ekta_Diwas%2C_at_Kevadiya%2C_in_Narmada_District_of_Gujarat_%283%29.JPG/250px-thumbnail.jpg" decoding="async" width="250" height="194" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/e6/Narendra_Modi_visiting_the_Museum%2C_Exhibition_and_the_Viewers%E2%80%99_Gallery%2C_during_the_dedication_of_the_%E2%80%98Statue_of_Unity%E2%80%99_to_the_Nation%2C_on_the_occasion_of_the_Rashtriya_Ekta_Diwas%2C_at_Kevadiya%2C_in_Narmada_District_of_Gujarat_%283%29.JPG/500px-thumbnail.jpg 2x" data-file-width="2012" data-file-height="1559" /></a><figcaption>Prime Minister <a href="/wiki/Narendra_Modi" title="Narendra Modi">Narendra Modi</a> in the museum within the complex</figcaption></figure>
<p>The <i>Statue of Unity</i> is the world's tallest statue at 182 metres (597&#160;ft). It rises 54 metres (177&#160;ft) higher than the previous record holder, the <a href="/wiki/Spring_Temple_Buddha" title="Spring Temple Buddha">Spring Temple Buddha</a> in China's <a href="/wiki/Henan" title="Henan">Henan</a> province.<sup id="cite&#95;ref-39" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-39"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>38<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup> The previous tallest statue in India was the 41&#160;m (135&#160;ft) tall statue of Lord <a href="/wiki/Hanuman" title="Hanuman">Hanuman</a> at the <a href="/wiki/Paritala_Anjaneya_Temple" title="Paritala Anjaneya Temple">Paritala Anjaneya Temple</a> near <a href="/wiki/Vijayawada" title="Vijayawada">Vijayawada</a> in the state of <a href="/wiki/Andhra_Pradesh" title="Andhra Pradesh">Andhra Pradesh</a>. The statue can be seen within a 7&#160;km (4.3&#160;mi) radius.<sup id="cite&#95;ref-ironman2.0&#95;10-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ironman2.0-10"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>10<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup>
</p><p>The monument is constructed on a river island named Sadhu Bet, 3.2&#160;km (2.0&#160;mi) away from and facing the <a href="/wiki/Narmada_Dam" class="mw-redirect" title="Narmada Dam">Narmada Dam</a> downstream.<sup id="cite&#95;ref-ibn&#95;1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ibn-1"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>1<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup> The statue and its surroundings occupy more than 2 hectares (4.9 acres),<sup id="cite&#95;ref-40" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-40"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>39<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup> and are surrounded by a 12&#160;km (7.5&#160;mi) long artificial lake formed by the Garudeshwar <a href="/wiki/Weir" title="Weir">weir</a> downstream on the Narmada river.<sup id="cite&#95;ref-41" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-41"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>40<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup><sup id="cite&#95;ref-ironman2.0&#95;10-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ironman2.0-10"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>10<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup>
</p><p>The statue is divided into five zones, of which only three are accessible to the public. From its base to the level of Patel's shins is the first zone which has three levels and includes the <a href="/wiki/Exhibition" title="Exhibition">exhibition</a> area, <a href="/wiki/Mezzanine" title="Mezzanine">mezzanine</a> and <a href="/wiki/Roof" title="Roof">roof</a>. The first zone also contains a memorial garden and a <a href="/wiki/Museum" title="Museum">museum</a>. The second zone reaches up to Patel's thighs, while the third extends up to the viewing gallery at a height of 153&#160;metres. The fourth zone is the maintenance area while the final zone comprises the head and shoulders of the statue.<sup id="cite&#95;ref-42" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-42"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>41<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup><sup id="cite&#95;ref-ironman2.0&#95;10-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ironman2.0-10"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>10<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup>
</p><p>The museum in the first zone catalogues Patel's life and contributions. An adjoining audio-visual gallery provides a 15-minute-long presentation on Patel and also describes the tribal culture of the state.<sup id="cite&#95;ref-ironman2.0&#95;10-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ironman2.0-10"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>10<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup> The concrete towers which form the statue's legs contain two elevators each. Each lift can carry 26 people at a time to the viewing gallery in just over 30&#160;seconds. The gallery is located at a height of 153 metres (502&#160;ft) and can hold up to 200 people.<sup id="cite&#95;ref-IT31OCT2018&#95;43-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-IT31OCT2018-43"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>42<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup><sup id="cite&#95;ref-44" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-44"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>43<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup>
</p>
<div class="mw-heading mw-heading2"><h2 id="Tourism">Tourism</h2></div>
<p>Over 128,000 tourists visited the statue in the first eleven days of its opening to the public on 1 November 2018.<sup id="cite&#95;ref-45" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-45"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>44<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup> It has been included in the <a href="/wiki/Shanghai_Cooperation_Organisation" title="Shanghai Cooperation Organisation">Shanghai Cooperation Organisation</a>'s '8 Wonders of SCO' list.<sup id="cite&#95;ref-46" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-46"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>45<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup> In its first year of operation, the <i>Statue of Unity</i> attracted 2,900,000 visitors and collected <span class="nowrap"><span style="white-space: nowrap">₹</span>82 crore</span> (US$11.64 million) in ticket revenue.<sup id="cite&#95;ref-47" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-47"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>46<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup> By 15 March 2021, 5&#160;million tourists visited the venue.<sup id="cite&#95;ref-48" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-48"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>47<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup> It was visited by 10&#160;million people by November 2022.<sup id="cite&#95;ref-49" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-49"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>48<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup> In five years since opening, the statue attracted over 15&#160;million cumulative visitors.<sup id="cite&#95;ref-50" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-50"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>49<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup>
</p>
